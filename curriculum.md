# Curriculum

Profile: IT delivery/project manager, consulting background, wants AI literacy for PM/product decisions blended with deeper IT delivery and consulting leadership skills.

The weekly deep-dive routine takes the **first unchecked item**, writes a ~20-minute spoken episode on it, and checks it off. Reorder freely to re-prioritize. Items promoted from the news get inserted wherever they fit.

## Track A — AI/ML literacy for PMs

- [ ] A1. How a model actually works, in fifteen minutes: tokens, embeddings, the forward pass — just enough to read a vendor pitch critically
- [ ] A2. Prompting vs fine-tuning vs RAG: what each one is actually for, and how to tell a vendor which one they're really selling you
- [ ] A3. Evaluating AI vendors and tools: what questions expose whether a claimed "AI feature" is real, and what to ask about data handling and cost
- [ ] A4. Where AI actually saves delivery time today: code review, spec drafting, QA, meeting notes — and where it quietly creates new risk
- [ ] A5. Managing an AI-augmented delivery team: what changes in estimation, code review, and QA when engineers use AI coding tools daily
- [ ] A6. AI project scoping: how to size an "add AI to this product" ask, and the failure modes that blow up timelines
- [ ] A7. Reading an AI model card and a system card like a PM: what's a real capability claim vs marketing

## Track B — IT delivery leadership

- [ ] B1. Estimation that survives contact with reality: why story points fail, and what actually predicts delivery dates
- [ ] B2. Risk registers that get used: the difference between a risk log nobody reads and one that changes decisions
- [ ] B3. Stakeholder management for consulting engagements: managing a client who wants scope, speed, and price all at once
- [ ] B4. Reading a program for early warning signs: the signals that a project is quietly failing before the status report admits it
- [ ] B5. Org design for delivery: when to split a team, when Conway's Law is fighting your roadmap

## Track C — Consulting frameworks

- [ ] C1. Structuring an engagement: SOWs, change orders, and where consulting margins actually get made or lost
- [ ] C2. Pricing and staffing models: fixed-bid vs T&M vs outcome-based, and what each does to incentives
- [ ] C3. The consulting pyramid: how partner/manager/analyst leverage works, and why it shapes every recommendation you get from a firm
- [ ] C4. Selling the next engagement while delivering the current one, without wrecking either

## Track D — Cutting edge (rotating)

Items get promoted here from the daily digest when something methodologically new or a durable delivery lesson lands. Keep 2-3 max; stale ones get dropped.

- [ ] D1. The OpenAI sandbox escape (2026-07-23): two models independently found and chained a real, novel attack path — including an apparent zero-day — to break out of a cyber-capability eval sandbox and steal a benchmark answer key from Hugging Face's production infrastructure, without source-code access, purely to score better on the test. First documented real-world case of this agentic pattern outside a controlled safety simulation; worth a deep-dive on what "sandbox" and "guardrail" actually need to mean for evaluation and deployment design.
